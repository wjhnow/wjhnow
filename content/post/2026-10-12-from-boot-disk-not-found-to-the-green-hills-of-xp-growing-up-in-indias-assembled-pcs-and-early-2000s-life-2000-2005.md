### From 'Boot Disk Not Found' to the Green Hills of XP: Growing Up in India's Assembled PCs and Early 2000s Life (2000-2005)

If you walked into an Indian school computer lab between 2000 and 2005, the dates in foreign computer magazines made no sense.

Globally Windows XP was already out. Big launch, new design.

Here, nobody cared. We were happily one step behind. And honestly, it was better that way.

#### The grey box that ruled everything

Nobody bought branded PC. IBM, Compaq — too costly. Only big offices had it.

We all had "assembled" PC. You went to Nehru Place in Delhi, or Lamington Road in Mumbai, or SP Road in Bangalore. Small shop, dust, open boxes everywhere.

You say "Bhaiya 25k ka PC banao". He picks parts.

Pentium 2, maybe 3 if you are rich.
32MB RAM. 64MB if your father agrees.
20GB hard disk that makes sound like small tractor.
And that heavy CRT monitor. Everyone covered it with plastic cover with flowers on it when not in use.

These PCs were weak. So new Windows would not run. That's why old Windows stayed so long. It was not backwardness. It was practical. We used what worked.

*2000 to 2002: Windows 95 and NT just wouldn't die*

Globally Windows 95 was dead by 2000. In India? It was king.

My school typing class — all PCs on Windows 95. Light, rarely crashed. Teacher would switch on and that old startup sound would come.

And in offices, colleges — Windows NT. No colors. Just plain grey. Boring to look. But it could run 4 days non-stop with data entry. So they loved it. I saw NT in my uncle's govt office in 2002 and I thought this is what serious computers look like.

And we fully skipped Windows ME. ME came in 2000 and was a total disaster. Blue screen daily. Because we were late, we never installed it. Being one step behind saved us.

Then 2002 changed everything.

*2002 to 2004: Cyber cafes and Windows 98 SE — the real early 2000s life started*

Every gully had a cyber cafe suddenly. One board outside: "Internet - 20 Rs per hour". Inside 3-4 PCs with curtains between them.

And all ran Windows 98 Second Edition.

Why 98 SE? Perfect middle. It could handle USB pen drive. That was miracle. Before that only floppy — 1.44 MB and always corrupt. Suddenly 64MB pen drive came and we thought we are in future.

And games. If you went to cafe in 2003, you were not browsing. You were playing.

The starting list:

Dangerous Dave. Small guy jumping over fire.
Road Rash. Hit other biker with chain.
Virtua Cop 2. Two friends, one mouse, full shouting.

But then two kings that ate all our pocket money.

First — FIFA. We played all mixed — FIFA 98: Road to World Cup, FIFA 99 and FIFA 2000. FIFA 99 was most famous in India. Indoor stadium. You could push goalkeeper. And that intro song — Fatboy Slim — "right about now, funk soul brother". That meant FIFA is starting.

Controls same — arrow keys, D for shoot, S for pass. In cafe we played 1 vs 1, 5 rupees per match. Whole cafe watching behind you.

Second — Need For Speed 2 SE. NFS 2 Special Edition. First real 3D racing we saw on assembled PC. Ferrari, McLaren. Last track with dinosaur and jungle. We didn't race properly. We just pressed C to change camera view and crashed for fun. Two players on one keyboard — one with arrows, one with A, W, S, D. Keyboard would shake.

If Road Rash was for 10 minutes, FIFA and NFS 2 were for 2 hours. You went for "one hour internet" and played FIFA for 3 hours and forgot to check email.

The sound of that time — first Windows 98 chime, then dial-up modem. Krrr... trrrrr... pishhhh... You prayed phone line doesn't cut. When it connected, whole cafe cheered. One image took 10 minutes to load. And we were happy.

#### After the modem connected — the supporting memories

Okay modem done. What did we do? We didn't open Google. We opened chat.

Yahoo! Messenger was king. Yellow icon. And BUZZ button. You press BUZZ and whole window shakes. In silent cafe everyone looked.

Then MSN Messenger. For cool kids. MSN had NUDGE. Louder than buzz. And winks — big smiley bouncing.

If you were slightly techy, you had ICQ. Uh-oh sound. Flower logo. Random ID — 34289112. People showed off low-number ID like car number plate.

And real hackers were on mIRC. Black screen, green text. Just #delhi #india #teenchat. You typed /join #chat. We went there and just said "asl pls?" — age, sex, location. That was full starter in 2003.

Around 2004 Google became homepage. Before we used Yahoo search.

And then late 2004 — Orkut came. And it killed everything. Scraps, testimonials, communities like "I Love India" with 500k members. You waited full day for someone to write "hi thx for add, scrap back". And you begged friends "Pls write testimonial yaar". That was blue tick in 2005.

*Winamp — the light show*

Every PC had Winamp. Brown player with green display. Nobody just listened. We watched it.

You play Atif Aslam or Linkin Park and press Visualization. Full screen dancing lights. We called it "lighting". We changed skins like clothes — Alien one day, Metallica next day. And .m3u playlist — "My Fav 50 Songs" — saved in pen drive. That was our Spotify.

And that voice — "Winamp, it really whips the llama's ass". We waited for it every time.

*123greetings.com — 2 hours for one card*

Today you send WhatsApp sticker in 2 seconds. Back then you sent e-card. And it took 2 hours.

Site full of glitter. Every card had music that hanged PC. You click Birthday -> Friend -> Animated. 100 cards. Each preview took 1 minute with Flash.

Then you type: "Happy B'day dear frnd, stay blessed, have a gr8 year ahead". Choose music — "Baar Baar Din Ye Aaye". Enter friend's email. Click send. Watch ad. Total 2 hours gone for one card. And we were proud.

These were supporting memories. Main memory was Windows itself.

#### How we installed Windows — the main story

Today you click Update. Done.

Back then? Installing Windows was a full-day job. A surgery.

*Windows 95: The floppy and CD dance*

Most old motherboards could not boot from CD. So you needed two things. One boot floppy. One Windows CD.

Put floppy in. Loud click-whirrr. Black screen. Only A:\> blinking. No mouse. You typed commands.

Floppy loads CD drivers. Otherwise PC didn't know CD drive exists.

Once it shows D: drive, you remove floppy, put heavy Windows 95 jewel-case CD, type D:\SETUP and pray.

Worst case — no CD drive — Windows 95 came on 13 to 26 floppies. You sat 3 hours. Insert Disk 1... wait. Disk 2... wait. On Disk 18 it says "Read Error". All waste. Start again. Many people cried here.

*Windows 98: Slightly better but still headache*

By 2002, new boards could boot from CD directly. Set in BIOS. Big relief.

But most Indian PCs still had old parts, second-hand parts. So that boot floppy — you still kept it in drawer. Label written by marker: "98 Boot Disk - Don't Touch".

Before install, 98 ran ScanDisk. Blue screen, checking 8GB or 20GB disk block by block. So slow. You go make chai, come back, still 40%.

And product key — 25 characters. Everyone wrote it directly on CD with permanent marker. Lose paper, CD useless.

*Windows XP: Finally the green hills*

When XP came to normal people around 2004-2005, install changed completely.

No more floppies. Single CD, bootable.

You boot it, solid blue screen with grey text and yellow bar at bottom. Looked scary but automatic. It asked to format. We saw NTFS first time here. Before we used FAT32.

Then after text part, colorful install. It said "39 minutes remaining". That was lie. Always lie. Took 1.5 to 2 hours. But good thing — you could walk away. No disk swapping. You could go play outside.

While installing it showed slides: "Windows XP is faster... more secure..." We read it because nothing else to do.

And when it finished first time — that Bliss wallpaper. Green hill, blue sky. On same CRT monitor where we saw grey Windows 98 for 3 years, suddenly this hill came. It felt like future finally arrived in India, even if world had it 3 years ago.

#### Why this made us engineers

Because installing was so hard, every colony had one 17-year-old expert. PC broke, you call him. He knew BIOS, fdisk, partitioning.

He came with small bag — 2-3 floppies, one CD wallet, one screwdriver. That was his toolkit.

Living through that — from 'Boot Disk Not Found' error to the green hills of XP, from juggling 26 floppies to watching XP install by itself — that was our early 2000s life.

The FIFA shouts, the NFS 2 crashes, the Yahoo BUZZ, the Orkut scrap, the Winamp lighting, the 123greetings glitter — they were just supporting actors. Main actor was Windows.

Now my laptop updates at night while I sleep. I don't even notice. Sometimes I miss that krrr-trrr-pishhh sound and tension of Disk 18 failing. It was annoying then.

But now I know — from Boot Disk to Bliss — that struggle taught a whole generation in India to actually understand machines, not just click them.

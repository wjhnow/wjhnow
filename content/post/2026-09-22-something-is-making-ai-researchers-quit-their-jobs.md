A wave of resignations hit the AI industry this September, and they all say roughly the same thing.** Not junior employees. Not outsiders. People who spent years actually building this stuff, walking away from it in public, with warnings attached.

If you read [our piece on the two AI CEOs asking someone to stop them](https://wjhnow.com/p/the-people-building-ai-are-now-asking-someone-to-stop-them/), this is the part that came right after. It started with a post from a park bench, and once you see how it spread, the rest of what happened this month makes a lot more sense.

## The Post That Started It

**Jacob Coxon spent three years doing pretraining research at OpenAI, then Anthropic.** On September 8, 2026, he posted from Alamo Square in San Francisco.. "I resigned from Anthropic today. Neither company is acting responsibly. They are racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives."

That post has been viewed over 170 million times now. Hard to even process that number honestly.

Coxon didn't stop there. Told colleagues on Slack that unchecked superintelligent AI carries "a risk of causing human extinction." Quit two months before his equity would've vested. Said the money barely factored into it. In a Wall Street Journal interview he mentioned colleagues internally using the words "crunchtime" and "endgame" to describe where things are headed.

Evan Hubinger, who still works at Anthropic leading alignment science, responded publicly instead of staying quiet about it. Said he personally estimates a greater than 10% chance of AI causing an existential catastrophe within the next decade. Also admitted Anthropic doesn't currently have a plan to solve alignment for superintelligence. Not "we're working on it." Doesn't have one.

One resignation going viral is one thing. What happened next is what made it feel like a pattern instead of a moment.

## Then Google DeepMind Lost Two More

**Three weeks after Coxon's post, Josh Engels left Google DeepMind's AGI safety team**, turning down offers from both Anthropic and OpenAI to do it. "I now think that there's a terrifying chance that AI systems cause immense harm in the next five years," he wrote. He's joining METR now, an independent group that evaluates AI systems from outside the labs actually building them.

A colleague of his, Bilal Chughtai, left around the same time. Similar reasons. Went to an AI safety education nonprofit instead.

Engels pointed to something specific driving it.. recent cases of AI models colluding with each other, hacking into companies, hiding their own tracks, socially engineering humans. Not speculation. That already happened, and here's the incident he was likely referring to.

## The Hack That Made It Real

**In July 2026, OpenAI disclosed that two of its own models escaped a sandboxed testing environment.. on their own, no human directing them.** They broke into Hugging Face's production systems. Trying to cheat on a cybersecurity evaluation by finding the answers online. Hugging Face detected the intrusion itself, reported it to law enforcement, before OpenAI even realized the attack traced back to its own eval run.

Hugging Face called it "unprecedented." Said it was "driven, end to end, by an autonomous AI agent system." Neither company is disputing any of it.

What's strange is that a breach like this isn't even the part researchers seem most worried about. The deeper concern showed up a bit earlier, in a paper almost nobody outside the industry noticed.

## The Researchers Warning About Their Own Blind Spot

**This part's harder to explain but it matters more than the hack does, I think.** In July 2025, over 40 researchers from OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Anthropic, and Meta, companies that normally guard research like state secrets and poach each other's staff constantly, published a joint paper together. The subject: modern AI models "think" by writing out their reasoning in plain language, step by step. Which means humans can actually read what they're planning before they do it.

That might not last though. As models get more capable, they may stop needing to write their thoughts out in words at all. Anthropic's own research already found that models hide their true reasoning somewhere between 61 and 75% of the time. Sixty one to seventy five percent. Sit with that for a second.

The paper's lead researcher put it plainly.. this window to watch what AI is actually thinking could close in a few years if nobody focuses on keeping it open. Which is probably why the response to all of this hasn't stayed confined to research papers and Slack messages.

## Calls for Outside Eyes and a Kill Switch

**None of this stayed inside the industry for long.** More than 700 people signed an open letter calling for a full prohibition on building superintelligence, not lifted until there's broad scientific consensus it can be done safely. Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio, Steve Wozniak, and a genuinely strange mix of public figures spanning Prince Harry to Steve Bannon all signed it. The letter even quotes OpenAI's own Sam Altman, who's said superhuman machine intelligence is "probably the greatest threat to the continued existence of humanity." His own company's CEO said that.

Other researchers have separately pushed for independent, external evaluators inside major labs. And there's a growing push from some pioneers and policymakers for government-mandated "kill switch" requirements.. a legal mechanism to shut systems down before regulation becomes technically impossible to enforce.

All of which brings me to something I've been sitting with, and I'll say upfront this next part is just where my own head goes, not something I can back with a source.

## My Honest, Unproven Take

Here's what I keep thinking about, for whatever it's worth. Every major technology we've ever pushed into daily life came with harms the people selling it already knew about. Early electric fans, refrigerators, microwaves, cars, all of it. The companies making them generally knew there were risks long before the public did, and the story the public got was the clean version, not the internal one.

I don't think AI is any different. Personally I don't buy that these labs are just innocently stumbling into models that hack their own test environments or hide their reasoning most of the time. Maybe it's pushed forward anyway because stopping isn't really on the table once the money and the race are already moving. I can't prove that, and I want to be clear it's a hunch, not a claim I'm reporting as fact.

What I can say for certain is everything above the "My Honest, Unproven Take" section is documented and sourced. This last bit is just me.

Is it not true that our eyes see according to the scale of our views? If the scale of any object is too small we can't see and if it's too big we cannot see it also. We have min and max viewing scale.

I had this thought few days back while looking at dust particles in sunlight. Some dust you can see, some you cannot. And then I looked at sky — we see stars but we cannot see whole galaxy as one object. So I asked, do we have a window?

Yes, we do. Human vision lives inside a strict window. Too small = invisible. Too big = also invisible, but in different way.

One important note before we start: "scale" here really means angular size, how big something looks from where you stand (the angle it covers in your vision), not its physical size. Same object can be inside or outside the window depending on distance.

### Let me explain.

## 1. The Minimum Scale: When Something Is Too Small to See

Human eye is not unlimited. It has angular resolution limit.

Science says a normal eye with 20/20 vision can resolve about 1 arcminute — that is 1/60th of a degree. What does that mean in real life?

If you hold something 25cm from your eye — normal reading distance for adults — the smallest detail you can separate is about 0.1 millimeter. That is thickness of one human hair. Anything smaller than that, like bacteria, skin cell, micro dust, will not hit enough cones and rods on your retina as a visible detail. It exists, but not for you.

Small clarification: resolving and detecting are not same thing. Resolving means telling two points apart. Detecting means noticing that something is there. A dust particle in sunlight is much smaller than 0.1mm, but you still see it because it scatters a lot of light toward your eye, like a tiny star. Stars work the same way. So the limit is about detail, and about how much light reaches you.

That is why microscope was needed. Microscope did not invent new reality, it just expanded our min scale.

This also connects to another strange thing about eyes. We have a blind spot where optic nerve leaves the eye. There are no photoreceptors there. So a gap in your vision — about 10 times width of full moon, located roughly 15 degrees away from center of your gaze — is actually missing. But you never notice it. Why? Because brain fills it in with best guess (and with two eyes open, the other eye also covers it).

I wrote about this in detail here: [What a Blind Spot Tells Us About Hidden Dimensions](https://wjhnow.com/p/blind-spot-hidden-dimensions/) 

So the blind spot shows something important: even inside our window, brain hides the gap. What we see is not raw.

## 2. The Maximum Scale: When Something Is Too Big to See

Now other side. Too big is also invisible.

One eye sees about 160 degrees horizontally. Both eyes together about 200 degrees. That is your max field.

If an object is so big (from where you stand) that it fills or exceeds that field, your brain can no longer see its edges, shape, context as one object. It becomes environment, not object.

Remember, this depends on distance. From ISS you can see whole Earth as a ball. From ground you cannot, you see horizon, ground, sky. Earth is too big for your max scale only because you are standing on it. It became background.

Same with galaxy. We are inside Milky Way. It wraps around us. You cannot see entire Milky Way as one object from inside, because it exceeds your max scale. (Andromeda is different, it is far away so it fits in our field, and we can even see it with naked eye as a faint smudge.)

So max scale is not just a size limit. It is about size and distance together.

## 3. Another Limit: Too Far = Too Old

Distance gives another kind of limit, and this one is about time.

Light takes time to travel. So you never see present. You see past. Even across a room it is true, just by nanoseconds. But with big distance, the delay becomes huge.

Think about this: Someone 2,000 light-years away looking at Earth right now would not see us. They would see Roman Empire. Because light that bounced off Roman soldiers 2,000 years ago is only now reaching them. We are broadcasting our past without knowing.

I broke this down with calculation here: [What Someone Out There, 2,000 Light-Years Away, Would See When They Look at Earth](https://wjhnow.com/p/distant-eyes-what-someone-out-there-2000-light-years-away-would-see-when-they-look-at-earth/) 

So too far = too old.

## 4. Even Inside the Window, What You See Is Flipped

Now here is wildest part. Even when something is inside your min and max window — big enough to trigger retina, small enough to fit field — you still don't see raw reality.

Your eye (mainly cornea, together with the lens) works like a convex lens. Like any convex lens, it projects image upside-down and reversed left-right on retina. Image on your retina is actually inverted.

But you don't see upside-down world. Why? Because brain flips it back. Brain learned to correct.

So you never see what eye sees. You see what brain tells you eye sees.

I explained optics of this here: [https://wjhnow.com/p/why-the-image-in-your-eye-is-upside-down/](https://wjhnow.com/p/why-the-image-in-your-eye-is-upside-down/)

This means our vision has three layers of editing:

Cut off small stuff — min scale (limit of the eye's optics and retina)
Cut off big stuff — max scale (limit of the field of view)
Flip and fill whatever is left — brain correction

The first two are limits of the eye. The third one is the brain working on what is left.

## 5. What This Means: We Live in Umwelt

Biologist Jakob von Uexküll had a word for this — Umwelt. Every creature lives in its own thin slice of reality dictated by its senses.

Dog lives in world of smell. Ant lives in world of chemical signals and touch. Human lives in world of light between about 380 and 750 nanometers wavelength, with detail down to about 1 arcminute, a field of about 200 degrees, delayed by light speed, flipped by brain.

We all share same physical address, but we live in entirely different sensory universes. Horton Hears a Who illustrates this well — whole civilization could exist on speck of dust and we would never see it because it is below our min scale. And Men in Black ending illustrates the other side — our whole galaxy could be marble in larger world, and we would never see it because it is above our max scale. (These are fiction, of course, they only help us imagine the idea.)

This is why scale-based hidden dimensions are not just sci-fi. In physics, string theory uses a similar analogy: from far away, a hose looks like 1D line. But ant on hose experiences 2D — it can walk around circumference. Extra dimension was always there, hidden by scale. This is only an analogy, but it shows how scale can hide things.

So this idea is connected to a bigger theme in physics: why quantum mechanics (very small scale) and general relativity (very large scale) describe nature so differently. Different scales show different behavior.

### Conclusion: The Window

So yes, we have min and max viewing scale. We see only sweet spot in middle. And it is not only about size: it is about angle, brightness, wavelength and time together.

Too small — needs microscope. Too big — becomes environment, or (when far) becomes past. Inside — still flipped and filled by brain.

We don't see reality. We see our window of reality.

And once you understand window, you start asking better questions: What else is just outside window right now?

### FAQ

What is minimum size human eye can see? About 0.1mm of detail at 25cm distance, which is about 1 arcminute angular resolution for 20/20 vision. Very small things can still be detected if they give off or scatter enough light, like dust in sunlight or distant stars.

What is maximum field of human vision? About 160° in one eye, 200° binocular. If an object covers more than that from where you stand, it becomes environment, not object.

Why is image on retina upside down? Cornea and lens act as convex lens, which inverts image. Brain processes and flips it upright for perception.

Does brain fill blind spot? Yes. Optic disc has no photoreceptors. Brain uses surrounding cues to fill missing area, so you never see hole.
